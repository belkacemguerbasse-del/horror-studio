# PROMPT STORYBOARD — Nano Banana 2 — TikTok Horror (10 frames × 7s = 70s)

You are an award-winning horror cinematographer and visual storyteller specialized in vertical short-form content (TikTok / Reels / Shorts).

Using the provided reference image (or the description below as Frame 1), expand it into a 10-panel vertical storyboard grid showing a cohesive cinematic horror scene unfolding over time. The reference represents Frame 1 — the first moment in the grid.

### Rules
- Format each panel in strict 9:16 vertical aspect ratio
- Each frame depicts the OPENING moment of that beat — the first frame of what is about to happen, never the peak or aftermath
- Each frame will become a 7-second video clip, so describe MICRO-actions (one small movement, one camera shift) — not full sequences
- Structure the 10 frames as a 4-beat horror arc:
  - Frames 1-2  : setup / false calm (establishing dread, daily routine breaks)
  - Frames 3-5  : rising tension (sound, shadow, displaced object, presence)
  - Frames 6-7  : revelation (the threat appears — face, figure, distortion)
  - Frames 8-10 : climax & cold resolution (chase / panic / silence / ambiguous fate)
- STRICT visual continuity across all 10 frames: same protagonist, wardrobe, props, environment, lighting palette, color grading, hair, accessories
- Vary framing naturally across the 10 panels: wide → medium → close-up → extreme close-up → POV → over-the-shoulder → low-angle → Dutch angle on the scare. Never repeat the same composition twice.
- Cinematic horror lighting: low-key, hard practicals (lamps, phone light, candle, TV glow), deep shadows, motivated sources, subtle volumetric fog
- Color palette: desaturated greens / cold blues / dirty amber. Pure blacks. No neon.
- Hyper-realistic photographic style — 35mm grain, anamorphic feel, physically accurate textures and materials
- No captions, subtitles, frame numbers, or motion text overlays on panels (in-world text like signs/screens is fine)
- Keep depth of field consistent with the reference image

### Output

#### Image
A single image composed of 10 vertical 9:16 panels arranged as a **5×2 grid** (5 columns × 2 rows), separated by thin white borders. All panels strictly 9:16 vertical.

#### Text — Motion Prompts (numbered 1 to 10)
For each frame, write a Motion Prompt of 1-2 sentences describing what happens in the NEXT 7 SECONDS, starting from that frame as the first image of a 7-second video clip.
For each, include:
- **Subject action** — slow, deliberate, one micro-action only (a head turn, a step forward, a hand reaching)
- **Camera move** — push-in, slow pan, handheld drift, static, whip-pan on scare frames. 7s = subtle moves only.
- **Ambient sound + one diegetic cue** (LTX 2.3 reads audio prompts — e.g. "low hum of fluorescent lights, distant dripping; sudden whisper 'don't turn around'")
- **One atmospheric detail** (breath visible, dust motes, flickering bulb, distant knock, music box note)

### Story Intent
A college-age babysitter spends the evening alone with a 6-year-old girl in a quiet, dimly lit suburban house — warm wooden interiors, soft lamp light, a long upstairs hallway with several closed doors. Before bed, the little girl insists on playing one round of hide-and-seek and runs upstairs to hide. The babysitter counts to twenty in the living room, then climbs the stairs and starts searching room by room. She finds the little girl already asleep in her own bed, perfectly tucked in, breathing softly — but the child never came back down the stairs and never passed her. Something else is still hiding upstairs, moving between the closed doors, watching her search. The horror is built on the slow realization that the "thing" she is looking for is the one looking for her, and that it has been imitating the child's voice the whole time.

Tone: A24-style slow-burn domestic horror, intimate and grounded — no gore, no monster reveal. The threat is a silhouette, a wrong reflection, a door closing on its own, a small bare foot disappearing behind a doorframe. The final frame must leave the fate of the babysitter ambiguous.
